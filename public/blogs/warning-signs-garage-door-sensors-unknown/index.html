Five clear warning signs of failing garage door sensors

Watch for these observable problems. Each sign lists a recommended immediate action.

  • Door reverses while closing
    Risk level high: stop normal use and perform a block test; if reversal persists, call a pro.
  • Door will not close at all
    Risk level high: check for sensor beam obstruction and LED status; if beams align but door still won’t close, schedule a technician.
  • Intermittent or inconsistent operation
    Risk level moderate: try cleaning lenses and checking wiring; persistent or increasing frequency needs pro service.
  • Sensor LED behavior is abnormal
    Risk level moderate to high: a flashing or unlit LED often points to misalignment, wiring, or a failed sensor module.
  • Visible damage or loose mounts
    Risk level high: stop fiddling with wiring; secure the area and arrange a professional repair.

How garage door safety sensors work (two-sentence overview)

Garage door safety sensors are paired devices mounted near the floor on each side of the door that create an invisible infrared beam. If the beam is blocked or the sensors fail, modern openers are designed to stop or reverse the door to prevent injury or vehicle damage.

Quick homeowner troubleshooting (safe DIY checks you can do in 10 minutes)

Only attempt low-risk inspections. If you are uncomfortable or find exposed wiring, stop and call a professional.

  • Step 1, visual inspection: With the opener powered, look at both sensors near the floor. Confirm lenses are intact, mounts secure, and no objects block the beam. This takes 1 to 2 minutes.
  • Step 2, lens cleaning: Wipe the lenses with a soft, dry cloth to remove dust or spider webs. Avoid sprayed cleaners that can enter the housing. Cleaning is effective in many cases and takes about 2 minutes.
  • Step 3, alignment check: Have a helper stand at the opposite sensor and look for the LED status while you gently nudge each sensor mount until the status LED changes to the aligned indication. Only adjust the mounting bracket, not the wiring. Allow 2 to 5 minutes.
  • Step 4, the block test: Place a small cardboard box or a piece of wood in the beam path while the door closes. The door should reverse immediately on contact with the blocked beam. If it does not, stop using the door and call a technician.
  • Step 5, inspect visible wiring: Look for crushed or chewed wires along the mounting strip outside the housing. If wiring is exposed or frayed, do not touch it. That is a call-a-pro condition.
Watch for this: Do not open sensor housings or work on energized wiring. Power to the opener may need to be disconnected by a professional before any electrical repair.

Frequently asked questions

What do lights on garage door sensors mean?

Sensor lights indicate power and alignment status. Typically, one sensor shows power and the other shows alignment. A steady light usually means the sensor has power; a flashing or unlit LED commonly signals misalignment, low power, wiring issues, or sensor failure. If light patterns are unclear, have a licensed technician inspect the system.

What is the most common garage door repair?

The most common garage door repair is spring replacement or adjustment, followed by opener and sensor adjustments. Sensors often require cleaning or alignment to restore safe operation, making them a frequent service call for safety-related faults.

What is the average lifespan of a garage door opener?

The average lifespan of a garage door opener is typically 10 to 15 years, depending on usage, maintenance, and environmental conditions. Regular maintenance of sensors and mechanical parts can extend opener life and prevent premature failures.

What is the average cost of repairing a garage door?

Average repair costs vary widely by the fault. Minor sensor adjustments or cleaning are low-cost jobs, while sensor replacement, wiring repair, or opener upgrades increase costs. A diagnostic visit clarifies the issue so you receive an accurate, itemized estimate.
